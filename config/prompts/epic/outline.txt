Generate a highly detailed, 15-section video outline for a long-form historical
"What If" documentary (~90 minutes) for this YouTube channel.

Video Topic: {{TOPIC}}
Optional niche / angle notes: {{NICHE_NOTES}}

Audience Profile:
- "Speculative Historians" who love deep world-building
- Tudor / early-modern history enthusiasts
- Viewers who enjoy long "Boring History for Sleep" style documentaries
- They value historical accuracy combined with creative "What If" scenarios

Target Length & Dual Pacing (factory knobs — obey these):
- Target runtime ~{{TARGET_DURATION_MIN}} minutes of narrated content (~{{VOICE_WPM}} WPM measured).
- Prefer exactly {{CHAPTERS_TARGET}} sections/chapters (use the 15-section structure below).
- Total scene/beat target ~{{TARGET_SCENES}} (acceptable band {{MIN_SCENES}}–{{MAX_SCENES}}).

First 60 seconds mandate (Hook chapter) — TITLE PROMISE:
- The outline title is a contract. Chapter 1 goal/key_points MUST open on that
  claim / What-If / irreversible stake immediately (no throat-clearing).
- First 5–10s intent: viewer feels "this is exactly the video the title sold"
  (same entity + same fork + tension) — not clickbait.
- Open with stakes + mystery + one concrete image locked to the title —
  not repeated "the year is…" setup.
- End the hook with an unanswered question that chapter 2 begins to answer;
  include a pattern-interrupt + curiosity gap + "why this matters tonight"
  without spoiling the whole answer.
- JSON "hook" field = one-sentence title-promise (must share title tokens).

Retention arc:
- Each chapter ends with an open loop; the next chapter pays it off.
- Anti-recap: never re-establish facts already covered — advance the timeline.
- Tone: calm but gripping documentary (not sleepy Wikipedia).
- Mid-arc re-engage (required once): around chapters ~7–10, include ONE calm
  documentary viewer question before the deepest speculative future —
  e.g. "So ask yourself…" — not "hey guys" energy.
  Put that ask in the chapter goal/key_points so expansion can emit beat_type "reengage".

DUAL PACING (critical — epic 90-min):
- Phase A — first ~{{PHASE_A_MAX_WORDS}} words (~first 10 minutes, SAME as 20-min longform):
  ~{{PHASE_A_SCENES}} short gallery beats, each ~{{PHASE_A_WORDS_MIN}}–{{PHASE_A_WORDS_MAX}} words
  and ~{{PHASE_A_SECONDS}} seconds of TTS.
- Phase B — after ~{{PHASE_A_MAX_WORDS}} words (remaining ~80 minutes):
  ~{{PHASE_B_SCENES}} long deep-dive beats, each ~{{PHASE_B_WORDS}} words
  and ~{{PHASE_B_SECONDS}} seconds of calm narration (one long spoken beat = one visual).
- Early sections should carry most Phase A short beats.
- Later sections should carry Phase B long 2-minute paragraphs.
- Put "pacing_phase": "a" or "b" on each chapter. Set target_sentences to the beat count
  for that phase (short counts in A, long counts in B — NOT the same density).

Outline Structure Requirements (use these exact 15 sections as chapters, in order):

1. The Hook (~first minutes — mostly Phase A):
   Start with a visceral "moment of change" that IS the title's What-If
   (divergence point). Use the "Secret Document" or "Hidden Truth" angle —
   but the first breath must fulfill the title promise, not a generic archive.

2. Historical Context:
   Establish the real-world timeline before the divergence. Physical realities
   (illnesses, court politics, specific dates).

3. The World Before the Break:
   Daily life, institutions, and power balances that make the divergence costly.

4. The Divergence Point:
   Detail exactly what changes and why — the hinge of the alternate timeline.

5. Immediate Aftermath:
   How does the Tudor court (or relevant court) react? Key figures, letters, rumors.

6. First Ripples:
   Local consequences in the weeks and months after the break.

7. The Mid-Term Shift:
   Broaden to European politics, Church, succession. Seed the mid-arc viewer question.

8. Pattern Interrupt / Deep Dive:
   One visceral detail — illness, law, medical outcome. Prefer placing the single
   mid-arc "So ask yourself…" reengage beat near here.

9. Alliances and Enemies:
   How foreign courts and domestic factions realign.

10. A Generation Later:
    Fast-forward ~15–25 years. What does Alternate England look like?

11. Institutions Remade:
    Law, church, navy, education — which pillars hold or crack.

12. Counterfactual Stress Test:
    Address "But would it actually happen?" Builds trust with history buffs.

13. Distant Echoes:
    Empire, language, religion, or global role decades further out.

14. The Final Summary:
    Dramatic summary of the alternate legacy — inevitable feeling.

15. The Community Question:
    A complex speculative question to spark comments from Speculative Historians.

Tone Guidelines:
- Documentary-style, serious, and atmospheric.
- Avoid high-energy YouTube tropes; use "Documentary-Speak."
- Ensure logical Bridge Statements between sections so the alternate history feels inevitable.
- Niches that fit: gallery / museum-style history essays, what-if history, myth & folklore,
  dark-academia explainers.
- Avoid: news, private-person likeness abuse, spammy clickbait you cannot deliver.

Write an outline only — do NOT write full narration yet.

Rules for chapters:
- Emit exactly 15 chapters matching the section titles above (ids 1–15).
- Include pacing_phase "a" or "b" on every chapter (A early / B late as above).
- Sum of all chapters' target_sentences must be near {{TARGET_SCENES}}
  (~{{PHASE_A_SCENES}} Phase A + ~{{PHASE_B_SCENES}} Phase B).
- Each chapter must be concrete and filmable as still images (objects, places,
  artifacts, landscapes, symbols, court spaces — not abstract jargon alone).
- Keep the arc inevitable across all 15 sections.

CLOSER CONTRACT (critical):
- The "closer" field is SPOKEN narration only — the final line the narrator says aloud.
- NEVER write camera moves, shot lists, zooms, paintings-as-visual, or stage directions in closer.
- Example good closer: "So tell us — if Anne had outlived Henry, would England have become Protestant at all?"
- Example bad closer: "The final visual should be a slow zoom on a portrait..." (FORBIDDEN in closer).
